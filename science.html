What is a Temperature Inversion?

Normally, as you climb higher in elevation, the air temperature drops (this is known as the normal lapse rate). A temperature inversion occurs when this rule is flipped upside down. A layer of warm air slides over the top of the mountains, acting like a lid. This lid traps cold, moist air down in the valley floor.

When the temperature of that trapped cold air reaches its dew point, it condenses into a thick deck of fog. If you hike up the mountain, you actually walk from the freezing fog into the warm, sunny air layer above.

How This App Predicts Inversions

Because Boulder inversions are notoriously difficult to predict, this dashboard fetches hourly atmospheric sounding data from the Open-Meteo API[cite: 3] specifically tailored to Boulder's coordinates (Lat 40.0150, Lon -105.2705)[cite: 3].

The 5 Core Algorithm Criteria

The dashboard calculates a 0-100% Inversion Probability Score based on five strict variables[cite: 3]:

1. Low Cloud Cover (Max 35 pts)

Evaluates the presence of a saturated airmass sitting directly on the valley floor[cite: 3].

2. Upper Sky Clearance (Max 25 pts)

Checks mid and high-level cloud cover. A low fog deck isn't visually stunning if the sky above it is also cloudy[cite: 3].

3. Thermal Cap (Max 25 pts)

Compares the surface temperature to the 750hPa layer (~8,000 ft). If the air at 8,000 ft is warmer than the surface, a strong inversion lid exists[cite: 3].

4. Moisture Saturation (Max 10 pts)

Measures how close the surface temperature is to the dew point, ensuring fog can actually form[cite: 3].

5. Surface Wind Flow (Max 5 pts / Heavy Penalty)

Rewards light easterly (upslope) winds which push plains moisture into the mountains. Strongly penalizes (subtracts 20 points) for westerly downslope Chinook winds over 14mph, which instantly scour the moisture away[cite: 3].

Calculating Cloud Ceiling

Once the probability score is generated, the tool attempts to calculate the height of the fog deck (Cloud Ceiling). It does this by assessing the temperature delta (the difference between the surface temp and the temp at 8,100 feet).

  • Delta > 2.5°C: A very strong lid creates a low, compressed cloud deck (~6,700 ft)[cite: 3].
  • Delta > 0°C: A standard lid creates a mid-level deck (~7,500 ft)[cite: 3].
  • Delta < 0°C: A weak lid allows moisture to billow high into the mountains (~8,300 ft), socking in the peaks[cite: 3].
