The meteorology and the limits

What we actually want

An enjoyable sea-of-clouds morning requires a low cloud deck over the Boulder valley, the layer to end below a reachable high point, and relatively clear air above that layer. A thermodynamic inversion (temperature increasing with height) helps trap moist air, but neither confirms clouds nor guarantees their depth. A Boulder airport ceiling describes the base of a cloud deck, not its top.

Inputs: issued NOAA HRRR numerical forecasts

Using indexed GRIB2 byte-range retrieval, the app reads cloud fractions at low/middle/high layers, forecast visibility, 2-meter temperature/dew point, wind at 10 meters, modeled boundary-layer height, cloud-base/cloud-top estimates, and geopotential height/RH/temperature/wind at several pressure surfaces. Numerical values are sampled from ~3 km grid cells, including a ten-point foothills transect. They are not direct observations.

The generic model cloud-top field can describe high cirrus and is not the top of the Boulder valley fog. The vertical humidity profile has sparse pressure levels and interpolates between them; a drying transition is a useful diagnostic, but it is not a measured low-cloud top.

One explainable area-wide score

The score in inversion/opportunity.py reuses a physically motivated shallow-cloud suitability calculation: low cloud near Boulder, cloud base not too high, dry air by roughly 7,400 ft and at/above Green Mountain, and a smooth penalty for mid/high clouds. It can additionally incorporate a modest modifier for measured model-grid variation along the valley and foothills. It does not create separate scores for individual mountain peaks. If required information is missing, the numerical index is unavailable, not silently zero.

The forecast score is a hand-tuned index, not a frequency-calibrated probability. The model's vertical resolution, terrain representation, cloud layer definitions and correlation between humidity and cloud cover limit precision. Historical iterations are frozen under their original formula versions.

Independent observations

We separately query IEM's aviation/METAR archive for KBDU Boulder, KBJC Broomfield, KEIK Erie and KLMO Longmont where available. These are surface reports of sky condition, visibility, wind and moisture, not cloud-height measurements above Boulder. They help check some of HRRR's predictions without turning a missing report into clear weather.

Honest forecast history

Predictions saved by the website before an event are captured; a prediction made later from a historically issued run is reconstructed. T−0 means latest run plausibly available by the event, not ground truth, and retrospective availability assumes a two-hour dissemination delay. The site never silently rewrites earlier archived scores.

Better data in a future revision

To verify the actual hiking result we need timestamped, correctly oriented summit or upper-foothill imagery; cloud-top remote sensing may also help, with caveats for layered clouds. Public camera feeds are useful only if the view geometry, permission and historical availability are established. Until then, the app should not present a validated scenic event probability.

Sources: NOAA HRRR numerical archive; Iowa Environmental Mesonet airport archive; NOAA HRRR model information.
